## Overview

The Path (径) is an unbuilt spatial proposal for Queen's Lawn, informed by research across the lawn and Dangoor Plaza at Imperial College London. It brings seating, partial shelter, a defined route and proposed play elements into a low curved landscape.

## Outcome

The team produced comparative foam/cardboard spatial models, 3D-printed seating models, full-size cardboard seating prototypes and final CAD visualisations. The architecture was not built at full scale.

## My Role

I contributed across the full process: site research, concept development, modular seating and architectural modelling, physical prototyping, user testing and final renders. The final proposal is shared work by Bdobe Outdesign: Simon Xia, Yuxuan Zhou, Cici Song, Ziqi Zhou and Simon Wang.

## Process

Discovery included a survey of 45 students and interviews with 10 students, 3 maintenance staff and 1 professor. Fifteen ideas became three developed concepts. A later co-design activity with 11 students and 1 professor compared spatial models and evaluated full-scale seating.

## Technical / Design Decisions

Eight of twelve participants preferred the lower Path over the elevated Loop. Nine did not consider a folding seat essential, guiding the design towards separable modules and a detachable head-support connection. Flooring, drainage and play features were developed in response to spatial feedback.

## Result

The final design is shown through renders and photomontages. Seating materials, charging, drainage, accessible circulation and play features remain proposal-stage design decisions requiring detailed development and validation.

## Reflection

The project connects decisions at the scale of a campus space and a person's body. User feedback changed both the architectural form and the furniture strategy, while highlighting the work still needed before construction.
