I disassembled the Braun MobileShave M60 to its smallest serviceable elements, numbered and weighed the parts, and reconstructed the logic linking product architecture, material families and manufacturing processes. Visual evidence, technician input, flame testing and CES EduPack supported the identification work.

The lifecycle audit showed that material production and transport dominate the product's first-life impact: 86.4 MJ of embodied energy and 5.47 kg CO₂, with the motor accounting for 68.6% of material-phase energy. This shifted the redesign focus away from use-phase optimisation and towards mass, material choice and recovery.

For a more industrial premium brief, I screened aluminium for the housing and titanium for the blade set, with die casting proposed for production. The proposal improves tactility, durability and recovery potential, while honestly raising first-life embodied energy from 86.4 MJ to 95.8 MJ. The project taught me to make that kind of trade-off visible rather than hiding it behind a single sustainability claim.
