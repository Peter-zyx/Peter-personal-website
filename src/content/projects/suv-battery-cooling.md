This project combined an SUV concept with an EV battery cooling investigation. The objective was to connect exterior form, aerodynamic performance, and thermal architecture in one coherent vehicle proposal, reducing energy demand while retaining a feasible engineering package.

My primary responsibility was modelling and CAD development within Group 23. The team combined concept development, hand calculations, CFD analysis and wind-tunnel testing. A PLA vehicle prototype enabled testing at 10, 20 and 30 m/s; the cooling study developed a parallel-channel plate with manifolds, tapered transitions and dual inlets/outlets.

The group report records a CFD drag coefficient of 0.286 and a wind-tunnel value of 0.31 at 30 m/s. Both methods identify the rear as the main separation region. The cooling CFD reports a mean battery temperature of 42.5°C, a maximum of 46.6°C, a 15.1 Pa pressure drop and 710.6 W of heat removal. These are simulation results for the stated boundary conditions; no formal mesh-independence study or physical battery test was completed. Further work should refine rear separation, coolant distribution and local flow losses, while reconciling the report’s analytical assumptions before making direct hand-calculation comparisons.
