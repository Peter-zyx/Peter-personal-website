I redesigned a three-seat economy-class row for short-haul A320/A321 and Boeing 737 cabins, asking how weight and waste could be reduced without sacrificing comfort, durability, affordability, or maintainability. User research highlighted soreness, poor sleep support, and the need to preserve economy-class accessibility, which led to a system brief covering ergonomics, a target seat mass below 10 kg per passenger place, modular repair, and clearly defined end-of-life material streams.

I developed the concept, requirements, Fusion 360 model, material strategy, disassembly sequence, accessory system, stakeholder map, and environmental assessment. The primary structure uses high-recycled 6061-T6 aluminium extrusions with a removable ribbed glass-fibre thermoplastic seat pan. Dual-density fire-retardant foam, breathable textile zones, and adjustable neck support address short-haul comfort, while the tray, cup holder, bag hook, USB-C power, and controls are mounted as independent serviceable modules.

The final concept estimates 10.6 kg per passenger place, 0.6 kg above the brief target, with a reported 41.1% mass reduction. Its eco-audit estimates 1,973 MJ and 129 kg CO₂ per passenger place, with reductions of 34% and 38% under the report’s baseline assumptions. The model excludes operational flight energy and does not establish a complete aircraft-use life-cycle footprint. Its Material Circularity Indicator is 0.686, supported by a long-life shared base, replaceable soft goods, accessible fasteners, and separation into aluminium, thermoplastic, textile, foam, and steel recovery streams. The project reinforced that sustainable design becomes credible when comfort, maintenance, product architecture, and environmental measurement are resolved together.
